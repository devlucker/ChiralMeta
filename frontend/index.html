Stereochemical Context: The Thalidomide Lesson & Rationale for Enantioselective Catalysis
Expand Context

The Mirror-Image Dichotomy: In pharmacology, non-superimposable stereoisomers (enantiomers) often exhibit diametrically opposing bioactivities. During 1957–1961, the racemic prescription of Thalidomide caused over 10,000 cases of severe phocomelia in newborns worldwide. Research subsequently revealed that while the (R)-enantiomer operated as a safe, effective sedative against morning sickness, its mirror-image (S)-enantiomer acted as a potent teratogen that inhibited fetal angiogenesis.

Asymmetric Catalysis Rationale: Modern drug discovery strictly necessitates enantiopure syntheses. Asymmetric hydrogenation catalysts employ chiral ligands coordinated to transition metals (Co, Ir, Rh) to selectively yield only one target enantiomer (enantiomeric excess ≥ 80% ee). ChiralMeta leverages Prototypical Meta-Learning to predict stereochemical selectivity prior to empirical wet-lab screening.
